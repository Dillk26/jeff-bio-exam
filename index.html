Unit 3 Master Study Guide & Diagram Reference

Everything checked off from Jeff's learning targets in one organized reference sheet.

1. General Organic Molecules & Carbon

  • Organic Definition: Contains both Carbon (C) and Hydrogen (H) bonded together. (Note: CO₂ and H₂O are inorganic because they don't have both C and H!).
  • Carbon's Atomic Stats (Atomic #6, Mass 12):
    • Protons: 6 | Neutrons: 6 | Total Electrons: 6
    • Valence Electrons: 4 (in outer shell) → forms 4 covalent bonds to reach a stable octet (8).
    • Can form single, double, or triple bonds and long chains, branched chains, or rings.
  • Dehydration Synthesis: Builds polymers from monomers by removing H₂O (forms covalent bonds).
  • Hydrolysis: Breaks down polymers into monomers by adding H₂O (breaks covalent bonds, e.g., digestion).

2. Carbohydrates

  • Elements & Ratio: Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen (C, H, O) in a strict 1 : 2 : 1 ratio (e.g., Glucose is C₆H₁₂O₆).
  • Shape & Suffix: Hexagonal/pentagonal carbon rings. Sugars end in -ose (glucose, fructose, sucrose, lactose, maltose).
  • Monomer: Monosaccharide | Polymer: Polysaccharide.
  • 4 Key Polysaccharides:
    • Starch: Energy storage in plants.
    • Glycogen: Short-term energy storage in animals (stored in liver & muscles).
    • Cellulose: Structural support in plant cell walls (fiber).
    • Chitin: Structural support in fungi cell walls & arthropod exoskeletons.
  • Photosynthesis vs. Cellular Respiration: Photosynthesis builds Glucose + O₂; Cellular Respiration breaks down Glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + ATP.

3. Lipids (Fats, Oils, Waxes, Steroids)

  • Elements & Polarity: Mostly C and H with very little O. Nonpolar and hydrophobic (water-fearing / does not dissolve in water).
  • Two Building Blocks (Monomers): 1 Glycerol + 3 Fatty Acids (forms a triglyceride via dehydration synthesis).
  • Types & Functions:
    • Triglycerides: Long-term energy storage, insulation, cushioning organs.
    • Phospholipids: Make up cell membranes (polar hydrophilic head + nonpolar hydrophobic tails).
    • Cholesterol / Steroids: Ring-shaped lipids; help cell membranes & act as hormones.
  • Study Guide Picture:
    • Straight chain (All single C–C bonds): Saturated fatty acid, packed full of H, solid at room temp (butter/animal fat). Linked to atherosclerosis / arteriosclerosis (clogged arteries).
    • Bent chain (Has a C=C double bond): Unsaturated fatty acid, comes from a plant, and is liquid at room temp (plant oils).

4. Nucleic Acids & 5. Proteins

  • Nucleic Acids (C, H, O, N, P): Store & transmit genetic info (DNA & RNA).
    • Monomer: Nucleotide = Phosphate group + 5-carbon Sugar + Nitrogenous Base.
    • Chargaff's Rules: A bonds with T (U in RNA); C bonds with G.
    • Gene: A specific segment of DNA that codes for a protein.
  • Proteins (C, H, O, N):
    • Monomer: Amino Acid (20 different types) → Polypeptide → Protein.
    • Sickle Cell Disease: A single amino acid mutation changes hemoglobin's 3D shape, causing red blood cells to sickle.
    • Enzymes (end in -ase): Biological catalysts that speed up reactions by lowering activation energy. Lock-and-Key fit between Active Site and Substrate.
    • Catalase (Liver Lab): Breaks down toxic hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) into water (H₂O) and oxygen gas (O₂).
    • Amylase: Enzyme in saliva that breaks down starch into simple sugars.
    • Denaturation: Extreme heat or pH changes the 3D shape of the active site so the substrate can no longer bind.
